Data Science life cycle

A practical summary of the NPL bundle for preparing, evaluating and improving tabular models

This block groups three short PDFs focused on the real working sequence behind tabular-data projects. They do not replace the original material: this page is a compact guide to navigate the bundle more easily, while keeping direct links to the PDFs exactly as they are.

The sequence is practical and consistent: first prepare the dataset and understand the metrics, then represent categorical or text variables correctly, and finally tune models, hyperparameters and ensemble strategies without losing sight of outliers, convergence and evaluation.

1. ML Accelerator Tabular Data1

The first PDF covers the foundation of the pipeline: data augmentation when real cases are scarce, data enrichment with new sources, variable transformations and the distinction between regression and classification problems. It also moves into metrics and validation, with notes on R squared, precision, recall, F1 score, bias versus variance, EDA, feature correlation, missing data, imputation and feature scaling.

  • Dataset preparation before model choice.
  • Critical reading of metrics and unbalanced datasets.
  • Correlation, missing values and imputation as part of the baseline.

2. ML Accelerator Tabular Data2

The second PDF focuses more on feature representation and modeling decisions. It includes warnings about column explosion during encoding, the need to use useful hierarchies such as city instead of zip code, text treatment with stopwords and Bag of Words, handling unseen values at inference time, and several notes comparing KNN, trees and sklearn techniques.

  • Categorical encoding without destroying dimensionality.
  • Text features with BoW and careful stopword handling when meaning shifts.
  • Robust inference with unseen values and practical limits of some algorithms.

3. ML Accelerator Tabular Data3

The third PDF moves into model optimization: combinations of hyperparameters, error minimization through gradients, learning rate, local minima and the search for the best weight vector. From there it connects naturally to logistic regression for classification, the impact of outliers in regression, decision trees, bagging, boosting and a final reference to neural-network architectures.

  • Tuning and gradient descent as part of the training phase.
  • Decision boundary and logistic regression to move from regression into classification.
  • Ensembles and more complex architectures once the tabular base is under control.
