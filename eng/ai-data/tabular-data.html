Tabular Data

The real working ground for most analytics and machine learning problems

Most real projects in business and healthcare start with tables: rows, columns, timestamps, categories, measurements and missing values. This is where the quality of the final model is decided long before training begins.

1. Problem framing

Before touching the data, I try to define the unit of analysis, the target variable, the decision that the model should support, and the metric that really matters. A good baseline often prevents a lot of wasted work later.

2. Data preparation

Tabular work is mostly about joins, filters, aggregations, reshaping and careful control of missing values. Categorical variables, duplicates, inconsistent identifiers and leakage can break a model faster than algorithm choice.

3. Feature engineering

The strongest gains often come from better features: time windows, grouped summaries, ratios, flags, domain-derived variables and more informative encodings for categorical data. This is where business and healthcare context pays off.

4. Evaluation discipline

I prefer train, validation and test thinking over optimistic one-shot results. For tabular problems it is also critical to ask whether the split respects time, entities and the real operating context.

5. Tooling I use or study here

  • R data frames as the base structure for analysis.
  • dplyr and tidyr for Data Wrangling and reshaping.
  • ggplot2 for exploratory analysis and error detection.
  • SQL, JDBC, ODBC and FHIR when the data lives in external systems.
  • GLM, tree-based models and other supervised methods once the table is trustworthy.
