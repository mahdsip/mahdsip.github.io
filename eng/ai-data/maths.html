Maths Foundations

Summary page with direct access to the full note files

This page keeps the maths section as a readable overview. The aim is to summarize the core foundations behind machine learning and data science, while leaving the full detailed material in separate PDF files that can be opened directly.

1. Basic linear algebra and vector spaces

The first block covers how data is represented before any model is trained. Vectors, matrices and tensors define the structure of observations and features, while transpose, addition, subtraction and vector spaces provide the minimum language needed to reason about data geometrically.

  • Vectors as ordered numerical structures.
  • Matrices as the working form of tabular datasets.
  • Tensors as the extension to higher dimensions.
  • Operations and vector spaces as the basis for similarity and distance.

2. Transformations and matrix models

The second block moves from representation into prediction. Rotation, matrix multiplication and changes of basis explain how data can be transformed into a simpler space. This is also the route into OLS, Eigen decomposition, PCA and principal component regression.

  • Regression-style prediction examples.
  • Geometric intuition for rotating data.
  • Matrix multiplication as the language of linear transformations.
  • PCA and related decompositions for correlated dimensions.

3. Probability and testing

This block introduces uncertainty through A/B testing, random variables and probability distributions. The goal is to connect product or business questions with statistical reasoning instead of treating testing as an isolated academic topic.

  • A/B testing as a decision tool.
  • Random variables as a model of uncertain outcomes.
  • Discrete and continuous probability distributions.
  • Statistical interpretation of observed results.

4. Likelihood and optimization

A large part of machine learning can be seen as selecting parameters that make observed data plausible under a model. That leads from maximum likelihood estimation to negative log-likelihood, derivatives, gradient descent and the optimization techniques used in modern ML systems.

  • Likelihood as a fitting principle.
  • Loss functions as optimization targets.
  • Derivatives and gradients as search tools.
  • Gradient-based training and automatic differentiation.

5. Bayes and information

The final block brings probability into classification and decision making. Bayes theorem, naive Bayes, fraud detection examples and information theory all help translate weak signals into clearer decisions under uncertainty.

  • Bayes theorem as belief updating from evidence.
  • Naive Bayes as a transparent baseline classifier.
  • Fraud detection as a practical use case.
  • Information theory as a language for uncertainty and signal.
